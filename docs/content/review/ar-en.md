## ar1 · التَّحِيّات — Greet people, say thank you and introduce yourself

### Guidebook tips

- **Right to left, joined up** — Arabic is written from right to left, and most letters join the letter after them, so a letter changes shape at the start, middle and end of a word. You read the first word of a sentence on the right. _مَرْحَبًا_ = hello; _شُكْرًا_ = thank you  
  Reviewer verdict: ☐ correct ☐ needs change — comment:
- **Short vowels (harakat)** — The small marks above and below letters are short vowels: fatha ( َ ) is a, kasra ( ِ ) is i and damma ( ُ ) is u. Sukun ( ْ ) means no vowel and shadda ( ّ ) doubles a letter. Everyday Arabic usually leaves them out. Speakara shows them on new words and accepts your answers with or without them. _كَيْفَ_ = how (kayfa); _جِدًّا_ = very (jiddan)  
  Reviewer verdict: ☐ correct ☐ needs change — comment:
- **You: man or woman?** — Arabic has a different “you” for a man (أَنْتَ, anta) and a woman (أَنْتِ, anti). Endings change too: حالُكَ (to a man) and حالُكِ (to a woman). _كَيْفَ حالُكَ؟_ = How are you? (to a man); _كَيْفَ حالُكِ؟_ = How are you? (to a woman)  
  Reviewer verdict: ☐ correct ☐ needs change — comment:

## ar2 · التَّعارُف — Say where you are from, which languages you speak and what you do

### Guidebook tips

- **No “is” in the present** — Arabic does not use a word for am, is or are in the present. Put the two ideas side by side: أنا طالب is “I (am) a student”. _أنا طالب._ = I am a student.; _هو مصري._ = He is Egyptian.  
  Reviewer verdict: ☐ correct ☐ needs change — comment:
- **The feminine ending ة** — Most words for women end in ة (ta marbuta), said -a. Add it to describe a woman: طالب (male student), طالبة (female student). _هي طالبة._ = She is a student.; _هي مصرية._ = She is Egyptian.  
  Reviewer verdict: ☐ correct ☐ needs change — comment:

## ar3 · العائِلَة — Talk about your family and describe people

### Guidebook tips

- **My, your, his, her** — Possession is an ending on the noun: ـي my, ـكَ your (man), ـكِ your (woman), ـه his, ـها her. A word ending in ة changes it to ت before the ending: عائلة → عائلتي. _أبي_ = my father; _عائلتي كبيرة._ = My family is big.  
  Reviewer verdict: ☐ correct ☐ needs change — comment:
- **هذا and هذه** — Use هذا (hadha) for a man or a masculine thing and هذه (hadhihi) for a woman or a feminine thing. _هذا أبي._ = This is my father.; _هذه أمي._ = This is my mother.  
  Reviewer verdict: ☐ correct ☐ needs change — comment:
- **Adjectives agree** — An adjective comes after the noun and takes ة when the noun is feminine. _أخي طويل._ = My brother is tall.; _أختي طويلة._ = My sister is tall.  
  Reviewer verdict: ☐ correct ☐ needs change — comment:
