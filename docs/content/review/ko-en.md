## ko1 · 인사 — Greet people, say thank you and introduce yourself

### Guidebook tips

- **Polite speech** — Most Korean speech uses the polite -요 endings (안녕하세요). The formal -ㅂ니다 style (감사합니다) is used in service and formal situations. _안녕하세요._ = Hello. (polite); _감사합니다._ = Thank you. (formal)  
  Reviewer verdict: ☐ correct ☐ needs change — comment:
- **Batchim links to the next vowel** — A final consonant (batchim) carries over when the next syllable starts with the silent ㅇ: 이름이 is said i-reu-mi, 학생이에요 is said hak-saeng-i-e-yo. _이름이 뭐예요?_ = What is your name? (i-reu-mi mwo-ye-yo)  
  Reviewer verdict: ☐ correct ☐ needs change — comment:
- **Particles mark the role** — 은/는 marks the topic and 이/가 marks the subject. Use 은 or 이 after a final consonant (batchim), and 는 or 가 after a vowel. _저는 학생이에요._ = I am a student.; _이름이 뭐예요?_ = What is your name?

## ko2 · 카페에서 — Order drinks and food and ask for the bill

### Guidebook tips

- **Object particles** — 을 follows a word ending in a consonant and 를 follows a vowel. Both mark the object of the verb. _물을 주세요._ = Water, please.; _커피를 마셔요._ = I drink coffee.  
  Reviewer verdict: ☐ correct ☐ needs change — comment:
- **Ask politely with 주세요** — Add 주세요 (please give) after a noun to order it politely. _커피 주세요._ = A coffee, please.; _계산해 주세요._ = The bill, please.
